# Somaliland minerals by region: research notes

Compiled 17 Oct 2026 from open web sources only (no dark-web or restricted sources). Public data is thin, dated and mostly reconnaissance level. Treat everything below as "recorded", not as a resource estimate, and have a geologist confirm before it is used in investor material.

## How reliable is the public record
- Much of it comes from British (1952-1962), UNDP (1962-1974) and 1975 stream-sediment work. The 1975 survey sampled about one site per 2 km, so its results are geochemical anomalies, not confirmed deposits.
- Hydrothermal vein occurrences have had little detailed prospecting in about 30 years (Kamenov and Petrov 2012, via the IJISRT review).
- Heavy mineral sands and coal are estimates that need feasibility work.
- Several gemstones (ruby, amethyst, tourmaline, topaz, jade) have no locality given.
- The Somaliland Central list of about 71 occurrences gives no region or coordinates for most entries and contains duplicates and mislabelled commodities, so it was used only for commodity names and host rocks.

## Awdal (well documented)
- Emerald and beryl: Borama is the centre of emerald digging (Gem-A 1999; Mindat lists beryl, emerald, garnet and phlogopite at Borama; USGS 2017-18 lists beryl ore, Awdal). Emerald sits where pegmatite meets dark micaceous schist; digging began in 1990.
- Gold: Awdal listed as a gold area (government investment page); "possible gold-bearing formations" near Faraxyada Hills and Borama (SOGERA).
- Copper and lead (investment page: grades 4-13% Cu and 30-60% Pb, reconnaissance and artisanal).
- Columbite-tantalite (investment page, Awdal and Sahil).
- Abdiqadir zone (Salal region, near the Ethiopia and Djibouti borders, 896 square km): government gold and base-metal block; reconnaissance survey by the Ministry.
- Opal: reported between Borama and the coast by analogy with Ethiopian acid volcanics (not confirmed).

## Maroodi Jeex (includes Gabiley area) (well documented)
- Sapphire in muscovite-rich pegmatite west of Hargeisa on the Gabiley road; garnet; emerald reported around Hargeisa.
- Dhagax-kuure / Dhagah-kurreh, Gabiley (about 160 square km): reconnaissance anomalies for gold, copper, lead, zinc; Dhagah Gure copper-molybdenum-lead-zinc veins in Precambrian porphyritic granite (UNDP 1975).
- Arabsiyo: nickel, chromium, zinc anomalies in Precambrian mafic and ultramafic rocks.
- Iron: hematite in Upper Cretaceous sandstone along the Hargeisa-Awareh road.
- Copper listed for Marodi-jeh on the investment page.

## Sahil (well documented)
- Gypsum and anhydrite: Daban Basin south-east of Berbera, surface estimates above 100 to 180 million tonnes, purity above 95% (investment page; African Mining Online); Damaan zone (120 square km). Sampling surveys with Khalifa Mining and Engineering and Ajham Energy and Mining.
- Limestone for cement (Berbera; cement plant was not operating at end 2018, USGS).
- Coastal heavy mineral sands: ilmenite, rutile, zircon, monazite near Berbera and eastern coast; Laalays zone (309 square km): gold and heavy mineral sand. Viability unproven.
- Coal: Daban Basin lignite (thin seams, quality varies by analyst); Hed-Hed valley south of Onkhor is the best coal (four seams, lignite to sub-bituminous); Gal Hamud and Guveneh hills minor. Not a recognised resource or reserve.
- Metals: copper, lead (Wiget, Dananjieh, Hulul, Bustanka, Ardahh are "near Berbera" prospects with galena, barite, fluorite), columbite-tantalite, magnetite, Fe-Ti oxides in the Sheikh gabbro (chromium and copper anomalies).
- Emerald (investment page), beryl at Ido Faito near Berbera.
- Oil and gas: Guban/Berbera basin; offshore blocks held by Genel Energy since 2012.
- Marble and granite: Berbera factory (USGS).

## Togdheer (partly documented)
- Nephrite jade: Somaliland Jade Mine run by Big Rock Mining, Al-Kairat Mining and African Mining Company (Ministry of Energy and Minerals).
- Copper listed for Togdheer (investment page).
- Fiqi Ayub district: a minerals exploration and extraction company opened offices in 2019; the mineral is not named.
- Oodweyne basin: oil and gas exploration block.

## Sanaag (partly documented)
- Tin: Assilih-Dalan-Dagah Kul area, eastern Sanaag; cassiterite in quartz and pegmatite veins and placers (1-3 mm grains). Dalan Central cassiterite and quartz-sulphide veins; Dalan North barite and galena.
- Gold: Laasa-Surad zone (288 square km) basement block; Sanaag listed for gold.
- Copper, lead, magnetite (investment page).
- Minor lignite at Har Araale and Ham-Ham, north and east of Erigavo (not significant).
- Eocene limestones (Karkar, Auradu) and Mesozoic sedimentary belt in the east.

## Sool (little public data)
- Eocene limestone plateaus and Mesozoic sandstone; Nugaal basin is part of the oil and gas exploration blocks (24 blocks, 180,000 square km, 5 billion BOE potential claimed on the investment page; Nugaal also extends into Puntland).
- No metal or gemstone occurrence was found in public sources for Sool. Note that the status of Las Anod is contested, so it is not shown on the map.

## Reported across Somaliland without a locality
Ruby, amethyst, tourmaline, topaz, spinel, zircon, jadeite, citrine, diopside, epidote, opal, manganese (rhodonite), iron, mica, feldspar (potassium feldspar tests reported), marble, quartz glass sand, asbestos, bismuth, molybdenum, niobium (pyrochlore, columbite), rutile, barite, fluorite.

## Round 2: data filed under Somalia, northern Somalia or British Somaliland
Searched BGS colonial survey records, a 2013 overview paper on Somalia's minerals, USGS publications and 2024-25 news. Nothing here is secret. It is simply catalogued under other names or is hard to find. The Kamenov and Petrov paper and the IUREP uranium report could not be read (the sites block automated access); their findings reach us only through the IJISRT review.
- BGS Earthwise (Somaliland Protectorate surveys 1947-1956): a government survey from 1952 mapped about 7,000 of roughly 8,000 square miles of basement; beryl, columbite and mica in later pegmatite dykes (a small company mined beryl); samarskite, allanite and monazite from pegmatite areas; a small cassiterite-rich vein in the north-east; frequent but uneconomic galena; manganese silicates, molybdenite, rutile; scores of millions of tonnes of gypsum-anhydrite 9 to 25 miles from Berbera; Shilemadu syenites at Nogal; oil seep at Dagahh Shabel; Pamphlet No. 1 (1954) on gypsum and anhydrite.
- Hussein 2013 (Minerals of Somalia): Dharkainle/Darkainle alkaline complex (about 33 x 3 km, nepheline syenite and carbonatite) north-east of Borama with apatite; rock salt at Zeila; gypsum-anhydrite south of Berbera (about 5 Mt gypsum, over 2.5 Mt anhydrite); piezoelectric quartz at Lafaruug and Elayo (Soviet work in the early 1980s, text garbled); copper-bearing quartzites at Bohl; tin and tin-tantalum at Dhalan, Majiyahan and Elayo (Erigavo district), mined by the British before the Second World War and by Bulgarian Technoexport in the 1970s; kaolin between Las Gal and Merodile; gold at Arapsiyo (13.5 ppm assay) and Abdulkadir; molybdenum and bismuth around Arabsiyo-Borama; platinum-group anomalies at the Mandhera complex and near the Hamar gabbro at Barkassan; simpsonite (a tantalum mineral) in beach sands east of Berbera; guano on Hiis and Mait islands.
- Lithium: Saudi company Kilomass reportedly secured an exploration licence in June 2024 (area not stated; Ministry could not be reached for details). Officials cite lithium potential, but independent surveys are limited. A social-media post claims an earlier Chinese licence in the west; unverified. Treat lithium as reported, not verified.
- USGS peralkaline granite report (OFR 83-389) covers Saudi Arabia only; no Somali data.

## Not found in public sources
- Lithium as a confirmed deposit, chromite as a named deposit, rare-earth deposits (other than monazite and allanite) and uranium were not found for Somaliland. Pegmatites and mafic-ultramafic bodies make them geologically plausible, but only the company's own data can support them. The map therefore lists lithium only as reported and unverified; chromium appears only as anomalies in Maroodi Jeex.

## Round 3: exact locations (USGS MRDS) and the mineral finder
- Source: USGS Mineral Resources Data System records for northern Somalia, read one by one from https://mineralresourcemap.com/detail/<id> (ids 10011781 to 10011896). 81 records fall in the Somaliland area; the raw table is in `data-raw/mrds.tsv` and `scripts/build-minerals.py` turns it into `src/data/mineralsMrds.ts`.
- Coordinates were transcribed by USGS from 1950s-60s Protectorate Geological Survey maps (Daniels 1960, Mason 1962, Hunt 1951/1958/1960, Gellatly 1960, Greenwood 1960, Farquharson 1924, Holmes 1954, UNDP 1970, Macfadyen 1952) and are rounded to about 2 km. They need field checking.
- Records at or beyond about 49E (Manja Vihin Magiaian, Arargob, Tomassah, Abderesach) lie outside the six regions shown, so they are listed but not pinned.
- Region assignment uses the generalised map polygons; the Berbera-Sheikh area (north of 9.95N, west of 45.5E) is assigned to Sahil.
- New minerals found at mapped sites: graphite, kyanite, talc, vermiculite, tungsten, strontium, bromine (hot springs at Bihen Gaha, Biyo Gora, Dubar, Hur), silver, fuller's earth, oil shale, uranium (Darkainle), manganese (rhodonite) at about 10 sites, asbestos (Qodli, Daarbuduq), barite and galena across Sanaag (Veliya Tug, Mokhor Holeh, Karin Kul, Karajiv, Unkah), copper at Gal Hamud, Elan Gubado and Fulanful, molybdenite at Dobo Well and Barkasan.
- Corrections: the Darkainle marker moved to 43.28E 10.40N (Awdal) and Dalan to 48.82E 11.08N (Sanaag), as the USGS records give.
- Tantalum: no mapped site inside the six regions. Recorded at Manja Vihin Magiaian (tin-tantalum, border zone) and as region-level reports (Awdal, Sahil, Sanaag). Exact positions are not published.
- Zinc and nickel are no longer listed for Awdal (zinc stays as a base-metal listing; nickel is not) because no source in these notes places nickel there; nickel stays in Maroodi Jeex (Arabsiyo anomalies).

## Round 4: company project areas and field records (Master File v0.2)
- Added `src/data/company.ts`: the 11 Awdal project areas, the Lughaya port site and the Darimile (Gabiley) tungsten prospect, each finding tagged observed, reported, unverified, inferred or confirmed as in the Master File. Positions are the average of each area's sample points.
- Company records appear in the mineral finder beside the USGS records, with their evidence badge and a note that they are not assays or resource estimates and do not imply a licence.
- The Master File places all areas except Darimile in Awdal. The generalised map polygons put Bacado, Alihayd, Gargara and Garbo-Teeb just across the Awdal/Sahil line, so the data follows the Master File and the map note covers the approximation.
- Left out on purpose: lithium, platinum-group metals, tantalum from the lab (not measured), the "diamond" claim, gold as a product, revenue figures and anything about licences.
